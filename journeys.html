    What is this?

    what3sounds gives every place on Earth an address, and lets many people say what that address sounds like.

    It grew out of what3chords, a satire of what3words. what3words has one word list, owned by one company, and presents it as the place’s address. what3chords copied that move with one table of guitar chords, so the chords came across as the place’s chords, when they are really the guitar’s chords applied to the place. The table hides who chose it.

    Here the two things are pulled apart. The address is shared: a point falls in a grid cell, and the cell becomes one large number. The number means nothing by itself. A codebook is a vocabulary that someone has contributed, and it reads the number as a few of its own sounds. At a given cell size the same spot always has the same number. It sounds one way through one codebook and another way through the next. Only the address belongs to the place. Each sound belongs to the people who wrote the codebook, and the page says who they are.

    Codebooks are not assigned by territory. Any vocabulary can be heard anywhere, so it is for its contributors, not this page, to decide whether that is acceptable for their material, and on what terms.

    What is here now

    One codebook is the guitar chord table of the original what3chords. The others are demonstrations written for this page so that there is something to hear: they are nobody’s tradition, and say so. Real codebooks need contributors. How to contribute one.

    What this does not do

    A web page cannot enforce anything. A codebook’s terms and Local Contexts labels are shown, and the page behaves as they ask (it will hide the download button, or leave a codebook off its list), but anyone can copy what a web page loads. Material that should be restricted should not go into an open web page at all.

    And the address layer is still anonymous: a global grid, applied everywhere from outside. You can change which grid, hexagons or pentagons, but not who drew it. Plural codebooks fix half of the view-from-nowhere problem. They make the other half easier to see.

    How journeys work

    A trip is a string of places

    Enter a start and an end. The route between them is a chain of grid cells, sized so the trip has a sensible number of stops and never finer than the vocabulary in use can tell apart. Each stop sounds the way its place does through that vocabulary.

    • Hex walk (or pentagon walk) is greedy local routing: at every cell, step to the neighbour nearest the destination. It runs entirely in your browser and cheerfully crosses oceans.
    • Follow roads asks the public OSRM demo server for a driving route and collects the cells along it. If the server is down, no road connects the two places, or a pin is far from any road, you get the walk instead.

    Playing it

    Press play (or the space bar). Tempo applies to every vocabulary. The guitar codebook brings its own band: a style, strum patterns, drums and bass. Other vocabularies sound the way their contributors set them up. Select any stop in the strip along the bottom to jump there.

    The share button copies a link that opens this trip through this vocabulary, at the version you heard. The download button saves the song as a WAV file, where the vocabulary’s terms allow it.

    On an iPhone, flip the ring/silent switch to ring if you hear nothing.

    Going live

    Go live follows your location instead of a planned route. The cell you are in sounds round and round until you cross into the next. Cells are about 50 m across on foot and larger when you travel faster, or as fine as the vocabulary can tell apart if that is coarser.

    The page keeps the screen awake while a session runs, because a web page cannot keep playing once the phone locks. When you end the session the route becomes an ordinary trip you can replay, and a recording can be downloaded where the vocabulary allows. Neither is kept once you close the page.

    This page does not send your location anywhere. Two things to know: the map loads its tiles for wherever you are from CARTO, as any online map does, and a link to the route is only made if you press share and agree.
